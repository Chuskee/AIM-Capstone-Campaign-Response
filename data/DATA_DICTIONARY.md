# Raw data dictionary

The supplied `marketing_campaign.csv` contains **2,240 records and 29 variables**. It is tab-separated despite the `.csv` suffix. This dictionary has one row per raw variable; observed ranges and missing counts were measured from the supplied file. An observed range describes this file, not a universal validation rule.

Source: [Customer Personality Analysis, published by Akash Patel on Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/imakash3011/customer-personality-analysis/data), whose data card credits Dr. Omar Romero-Hernandez. The source lists CC0: Public Domain. Descriptions below are concise paraphrases of the data card and original project dictionary; observed values and cautions come from inspection of the supplied file. Currency, geography, sample design and campaign timestamps are not established. The two `Z_` fields are not explained in that data card.

## Interpretation and modelling rules

- `ID` identifies a supplied record and is never a predictor. All 2,240 raw IDs are distinct, and there are no exact repeated records. Repeated measured profiles with different IDs are retained; a profile key excluding `ID`, `Response` and the two constant `Z_` fields keeps matching profiles together during every modelling split. The 2,236 eligible records form 2,034 predictor-profile groups. This controls train/validation overlap without claiming those records are the same customer.
- The response target is never used to construct a profile key, transform predictors, select features outside training folds, or form unsupervised segments. Summary plots involving `Response` are explicitly descriptive.
- Fixed eligibility rules require birth year 1920–1996 inclusive and income strictly above 0 and below 600,000 or missing. Three birth-year records and one income record are excluded, leaving 2,236 records. Rare relationship labels are mapped to `Unknown` without dropping their four records; `Alone` becomes `Single`. All 24 missing incomes remain. These rules are documented modelling assumptions, not source-confirmed errors. Learned quantities such as medians, clipping limits, scaling and feature selection belong inside the fitted pipeline. The cleaning log records every exclusion.
- Spend-to-income and spend-per-purchase are engineered ratios with mismatched or unspecified measurement windows. They are descriptive intensity proxies, not audited budget shares or accounting average-order values.
- Customer age and tenure refer to a documented historical snapshot. Using the current calendar year would silently change features in future reruns.
- Income, age, education and household composition may be sensitive attributes or proxies. The source has no gender or race variable; missing dimensions limit the scope of the fairness audit.
- Scenario value is calculated as `11 × observed responders contacted − 3 × contacts`. This is an illustrative accounting scenario. It excludes unverified costs, ignores treatment effects and does not establish causal uplift, incremental profit, or realised business ROI.
